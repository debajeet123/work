About Me

Debajeet Barman is a geophysicist exploring Earth's interior using seismic imaging, ambient noise tomography, and AI-powered inversion techniques. With a Ph.D. from Baylor University and field collaborations across Texas and beyond, I bridge theory and fieldwork. My research blends high-performance computing, machine learning, and seismic wave modeling to extract structure from noise. I believe science is most powerful when shared—through tools, open data, and visual storytelling.

Research Focus

I specialize in advanced seismic imaging and inversion methods that blend classical geophysics with cutting-edge computational tools. My work aims to push the boundaries of how we understand the Earth's subsurface using: Ambient Noise and Active Seismic Tomography Developed TensorFlow-based ambient noise tomography integrated with MUSIC algorithm, enhancing weak seismic source resolution. Full Waveform Inversion (FWI) Designed adjoint-free and Hamiltonian operator-based inversion methods that improve efficiency and model convergence. Machine Learning in Geophysics Applied GANs, VAEs, deep reinforcement learning, and physics-informed neural networks to solve seismic inverse problems with reduced data dependency. Current Projects High-Resolution Active Seismic Imaging Employing controlled sources and machine learning at Baylor University to build improved seismic velocity models. Unsupervised Inversion Frameworks Building scalable inversion pipelines using probabilistic and unsupervised learning approaches with HPC acceleration. Hybrid Inversion Models Integrating physics-based inversion with deep learning to enable interpretable and automated seismic imaging workflows. 🌍 Research Vision Scalable, Automated Seismic Imaging Creating robust, HPC-enabled pipelines for real-time seismic analysis. Earthquake Hazard & Resource Assessment Bridging academic innovation and societal impact through collaborations in geophysics, machine learning, and Earth sciences. Interdisciplinary Innovation Partnering across domains to explore new computational geoscience frontiers—from earthquake forecasting to sustainable resource exploration.
