About Me

Debajeet Barman is a geophysicist exploring Earth's interior using seismic imaging, ambient noise tomography, and AI-powered inversion techniques. With a Ph.D. from Baylor University and field collaborations across Texas and beyond, I bridge theory and fieldwork. My research blends high-performance computing, machine learning, and seismic wave modeling to extract structure from noise. I believe science is most powerful when shared—through tools, open data, and visual storytelling.

Research Timeline

2020–2021

Developed adjoint-free full waveform inversion (FWI) using Hamiltonian operator theory and matrix exponentials. Implemented elastic modeling via CUDA/MPI for scalable GPU performance.

2021–2022

Built a TensorFlow-based ambient noise tomography pipeline integrated with the MUSIC algorithm, boosting resolution of weak seismic sources.

2022

Led field deployments in Monahans (TX), Sweetwater (TX), and Fallon geothermal (NV). Integrated DAS and microgravity data for joint inversions.

2023

Applied GANs, VAEs, and temporal CNNs for unsupervised seismic inversion and physics-informed neural networks for enhanced generalization.

2024–Present

Developing real-time geospatial visualization tools with WebGL/Three.js and a browser-native seismic feature extraction toolkit.
